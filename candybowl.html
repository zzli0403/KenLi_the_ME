Reimagined Candy Bowl

Magnet-Powered Mechanical Candy Dispenser

ME 493 • Completed Project

Project Overview

The Reimagined Candy Bowl is a mechanically driven candy dispenser designed to spark curiosity and engagement through an unconventional actuation method. Rather than relying on motors or servos, the system uses a passive magnet-repulsion shooting mechanism to dispense a single candy per activation, emphasizing reliability, simplicity, and repeatability.

Developed as part of ME 493, the project challenged our team to design a system that met strict physical and performance constraints while showcasing core mechanical principles through an interactive user experience.

Problem Statement

Traditional candy dispensers often depend on complex electronics or continuous motor actuation, increasing failure risk and maintenance requirements. The challenge was to design a compact, durable system that could reliably dispense a single candy on demand while surviving frequent user interaction and handling.

  • Encourage engagement through mechanical novelty
  • Minimize reliance on active electronics
  • Ensure consistent, repeatable dispensing behavior

Design Requirements

  • Fit within a 15 × 11 × 11 in envelope and weigh under 20 lb
  • Survive a 3 ft drop onto a hard surface without functional failure
  • Achieve >90% successful dispense rate
  • Dispense one candy within 2 seconds of activation
  • Accommodate standard Starburst candy geometry
  • Use a common mechanical principle in an uncommon way

System Architecture

The system centers on a magnet-repulsion launcher that stores potential energy without springs or motors. A controlled release mechanism converts this stored energy into a short, repeatable impulse that ejects a single candy from the tray.

  • Magnet-based shooter providing consistent impulse force
  • Redesigned candy tray that both retains and guides candy during launch
  • Button-based actuation replacing an earlier gear-linkage concept

My Contributions

  • Redesigned the user actuation system after identifying accuracy issues in a long-lever gear linkage
  • Developed an improved candy tray geometry to prevent jamming during launch
  • Performed CAD modeling and iterative 3D-printed prototyping
  • Conducted functional testing, jam testing, and drop survivability evaluation

Validation & Results

Multiple rounds of testing were conducted to assess dispensing reliability and mechanical robustness. The final configuration demonstrated consistent single-candy dispensing and maintained functionality after repeated handling and drop tests.

  • Dispense success rate exceeding 90% under normal operation
  • No structural or functional failures observed during drop testing
  • Significant reduction in jamming after tray redesign

Key Lessons Learned

  • Mechanical simplicity often improves reliability more than added control complexity
  • Long lever arms amplify small tolerances and can degrade actuation precision
  • Designing for repeatability is as critical as achieving initial functionality
